# Copywriting frameworks

Each entry gives the stages, what each stage must say, the sections that usually carry it, when the framework fits, when it does not and the usual mistakes. The same data lives in `scripts/lib/copy-frameworks.mjs`, which the picker uses; if they differ, the script is the contract and this file is corrected. A framework is a starting order, not a template: on a short page two stages may share a section, and a stage may stretch over two.

Sections are the closed set `hero`, `problem`, `solution`, `features`, `how-it-works`, `benefits`, `proof`, `about`, `offer`, `faq`, `final-cta`.

## aida: Attention, Interest, Desire, Action

The all-round order for a visitor who does not know you yet and has to be won, then asked.

| Stage         | Says                                                                                            | Sections                        |
| ------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------- |
| **A**ttention | The one thing the visitor cares about, in a specific headline and visual                        | hero                            |
| **I**nterest  | That you understand their situation, and how the offer works, in concrete detail                | problem, how-it-works, features |
| **D**esire    | Why they will want it: concrete benefits, real proof, a picture of life with it                 | benefits, proof                 |
| **A**ction    | One next step, with the terms of the offer                                                      | offer, final-cta                |

- **Fits:** a stranger, an event, a product, a purchase or a booking, a visitor who knows the problem and compares solutions.
- **Avoid when:** the visitor already knows the product (it re-explains) or the offer needs heavy objection handling (it has no stage for it).
- **Mistakes:** a headline any business could print; ending at desire and burying the action; Desire made of adjectives instead of specifics.

## pas: Problem, Agitate, Solution

Name a pain the visitor already feels, show what it costs if it stays, then offer the way out.

| Stage        | Says                                                                                    | Sections                                 |
| ------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| **P**roblem  | The problem in the visitor's own words, so they recognise themselves                    | hero, problem                            |
| **A**gitate  | What it costs them if it stays: time, money, stress. Factual, never frightening         | problem                                  |
| **S**olution | The offer as the way out, how it works, what to do next                                 | solution, how-it-works, offer, final-cta |

- **Fits:** a visitor who knows the problem but not the solutions; services; a lead or booking goal; short pages.
- **Avoid when:** the pain is not the customer's to describe, or in sensitive niches where agitating a fear would be manipulation.
- **Mistakes:** a pain the customer never described; agitating until the page feels manipulative; a Solution that is only a slogan.

## fab: Feature, Advantage, Benefit

Turn each thing the offer has into what it does and what the buyer gains.

| Stage         | Says                                                                                  | Sections             |
| ------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------- |
| **F**eature   | A plain fact about the offer: what it is, includes or does                            | features             |
| **A**dvantage | What that feature does better or differently than the alternatives                    | features, how-it-works |
| **B**enefit   | What the buyer gets, in their terms: time saved, a worry removed, a result they value | benefits, offer      |

- **Fits:** a visitor who knows the type of solution and compares products; physical or digital products; a purchase goal; small and mid prices.
- **Avoid when:** the offer is content or a story (features feel cold) or the visitor does not yet see the problem.
- **Mistakes:** stopping at the feature; benefits that are features reworded; an advantage that names a competitor claim nobody can back.

## bab: Before, After, Bridge

Show life with the problem, life without it, and the offer as the bridge.

| Stage        | Says                                                                | Sections                                 |
| ------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| **B**efore   | The visitor's present situation and its frustrations                | hero, problem                            |
| **A**fter    | The situation once solved, in concrete everyday terms               | benefits                                 |
| **B**ridge   | The offer as the way across: what it is, how it works, the next step | solution, how-it-works, offer, final-cta |

- **Fits:** a visitor who knows solutions exist and needs to picture the change; services and content; a transformation the customer can describe truthfully.
- **Avoid when:** the niche is regulated (health, dental, legal, finance, supplements). The After would drift into an outcome promise. If used anyway, After describes the service, never a result.
- **Mistakes:** an After nobody can back; a Bridge that is only a tagline.

## 4p: Picture, Promise, Prove, Push

Paint the situation, state what the offer delivers, back it, then ask.

| Stage       | Says                                                                                   | Sections               |
| ----------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------- |
| **P**icture | A vivid scene of the visitor's situation or the life they want                         | hero, problem          |
| **P**romise | What the offer delivers, kept to what the customer can stand behind                    | solution, benefits     |
| **P**rove   | The promise backed with real proof: testimonials, figures, credentials, process        | proof, how-it-works    |
| **P**ush    | The ask, with a true reason to act now, and the last doubt removed                     | offer, faq, final-cta  |

- **Fits:** a visitor who knows the product and weighs it; mid and high prices; objections to answer; real proof available.
- **Avoid when:** regulated niches (the Promise stage becomes the risk: describe the service only) or when there is no proof and none can be built from process.
- **Mistakes:** a Promise the Prove stage cannot back; proof that is generic praise; a Push with invented urgency.

## pastor: Problem, Amplify, Story and solution, Testimony, Offer, Response

The long order for a considered purchase or a service people hire.

| Stage                  | Says                                                                    | Sections               |
| ---------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------- |
| **P**roblem            | The problem and the person who has it                                   | hero, problem          |
| **A**mplify            | The consequences of leaving it, and what solving it would open up       | problem, benefits      |
| **S**tory and solution | How the solution came about and why it works                            | about, solution, how-it-works |
| **T**estimony          | Real people or facts that it worked, from the brief only                | proof                  |
| **O**ffer              | Exactly what is offered, at what price, under what terms                | offer                  |
| **R**esponse           | What to do next, and the last objections answered                       | faq, final-cta         |

- **Fits:** high prices, many objections, services and lead or booking goals, a visitor who knows the problem, real proof available.
- **Avoid when:** a cheap or simple offer (too long) or when there is no story or testimony to tell truthfully.
- **Mistakes:** length without a point; a Testimony stage with invented quotes; Amplify that frightens.

## quest: Qualify, Understand, Educate, Stimulate, Transition

Address the right visitor, show you get them, teach, raise the wish, move to action.

| Stage         | Says                                                                                 | Sections                       |
| ------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------ |
| **Q**ualify   | Who the page is for, so the right visitor feels addressed and the wrong one leaves   | hero                           |
| **U**nderstand | That you know their situation and what they have tried                              | problem                        |
| **E**ducate   | What they need to know to choose well: how it works, what to look for, what to expect | solution, how-it-works, faq    |
| **S**timulate | The wish to act: benefits, proof, a clear picture of the result                      | benefits, proof                |
| **T**ransition | The next step, with the offer and one call to action                                | offer, final-cta               |

- **Fits:** a visitor who does not yet see the problem or the category; content and teaching offers; objections to answer; lead goals.
- **Avoid when:** an impulse purchase (it explains too much).
- **Mistakes:** educating at length with no path to the action; a Qualify so broad it excludes nobody.

## acca: Awareness, Comprehension, Conviction, Action

A lean order for a visitor who already knows the product.

| Stage             | Says                                                  | Sections                    |
| ----------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------- |
| **A**wareness     | What this is and that it is for them                  | hero                        |
| **C**omprehension | What it is and how it works, simply                   | features, how-it-works      |
| **C**onviction    | Proof, specifics and answers to doubts                | proof, benefits, faq        |
| **A**ction        | The ask, with clear terms                             | offer, final-cta            |

- **Fits:** a visitor who knows the product or the brand (a returning or referred visitor); a purchase or booking goal; mid and high prices; real proof.
- **Avoid when:** the visitor has to be introduced to the problem first.
- **Mistakes:** re-explaining basics to someone who needs the terms; Conviction with nothing specific to point at.

## story: Star, Story, Solution

A character the visitor identifies with, what happened to them, the solution.

| Stage        | Says                                                                                           | Sections       |
| ------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------- |
| **Star**     | The person the visitor identifies with: a customer, or the founder when the business is a person | hero, about    |
| **Story**    | What happened to them: the struggle and the turning point, in specific, true detail            | problem, about |
| **Solution** | How the offer solves it, and what to do next                                                   | solution, offer, final-cta |

- **Fits:** a visitor who does not yet know the problem or the category; personal brands, content, a founder with a true origin story; real proof helps.
- **Avoid when:** a low-price impulse offer (the story costs more attention than the sale is worth), or when the only available "customer story" would be invented.
- **Mistakes:** an invented character presented as a real customer; a story with no solution at the end.

## slap: Stop, Look, Act, Purchase

The shortest order, for a simple, low-risk offer and a visitor ready to decide.

| Stage        | Says                                                       | Sections          |
| ------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------- |
| **S**top     | A bold, specific headline that catches the eye             | hero              |
| **L**ook     | One or two reasons to keep looking: the key benefit, what it is | benefits, features |
| **A**ct      | Easy and clear action: price, terms, one button            | offer             |
| **P**urchase | A last reassurance and the call to action again            | faq, final-cta    |

- **Fits:** low prices, a purchase goal, products and events, a visitor who is nearly decided.
- **Avoid when:** a high price or many objections (it leaves them unanswered).
- **Mistakes:** a one-screen order for a high-risk offer; dropping price and terms to keep it short.
