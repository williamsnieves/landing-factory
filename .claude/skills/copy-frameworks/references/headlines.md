# Testing a hero headline with 4U

A headline is one sentence that has to earn the next ten seconds. Write two or three candidates in the page language, score each on four questions, and keep the best. The score is a way to argue about words, not a measurement.

| Criterion          | Question                                                       | Score it 0 to 2 as follows                                                                                       |
| ------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Useful**         | Does it tell the visitor something they want or need?          | 0 names only the business, 1 names a general good, 2 names a specific thing this visitor is looking for          |
| **Urgent**         | Is there a true reason to read and act now?                    | 0 none (the normal case), 1 a real, mild reason (a season, a deadline the customer gave), 2 a hard, real limit |
| **Unique**         | Could a competitor print the same line unchanged?              | 0 yes, 1 partly, 2 only this business could say it                                                               |
| **Ultra-specific** | Does it carry a concrete detail: a number, a place, a thing?   | 0 abstract, 1 one concrete word, 2 a concrete detail that comes from the brief                                   |

## Rules

- Urgency scores only when it is true and the customer gave it. A page with no real urgency scores 0 there and that is fine: do not invent a deadline to lift a score.
- A specific detail must come from the brief. A made-up number is not specificity, it is a claim, and the final check looks for it.
- The headline obeys the same limits as the rest of the copy: no promised result, no time-boxed result, no absolute superlative, no health or money promise.
- Three candidates should differ in approach (what the visitor gets, the situation they are in, the way the business works), not in one word.
- Read the winner aloud next to the first line under it. The pair should make one thought.

## Recording it

In the Copy plan, one row per candidate: the line, four scores, the total and a short reason. Mark the chosen one. Ties go to the more specific line.
